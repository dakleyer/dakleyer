# Cost of Clarity — EIS Estonia RUP applied-research project

> **Current applied-research project under preliminary programme assessment. This page does not claim a funding award, EIS validation, institutional endorsement or a concluded TalTech contract.**

**Project document:** [The Cost of Clarity — Scientific Problem and Applied-Research Method (PDF)](cost-of-clarity/cost-of-clarity-scientific-problem-and-method.pdf)

## The project

OÜ JUBAP has submitted **Cost of Clarity** for preliminary assessment under Estonia's [Programme for Applied Research (RUP), administered by EIS](https://eis.ee/en/services/programme-for-applied-research/).

The research question is:

> Can initiative-specific information conditions that are absent, contradictory, tacit or institutionally undeclared be identified economically before commitment, and can a sealed pre-commitment reading anticipate the later information-acquisition burden or initiative outcomes?

The intended output is an auditable **Data and Information Readiness Instrument**. It would help a decision-maker distinguish information that is available, tacit or distributed, contradictory or uncertain, institutionally undeclared, or genuinely unknown; connect each condition to evidence, authority and ownership; and produce a traceable position before commitment.

The first operating mode is deliberately expert-led. The proposed research design uses preregistered hypotheses and coding rules, separation between sources used to construct and test the criteria, inter-assessor reliability checks, comparison with simpler baselines, and sealed prospective readings made before advice or outcomes are known. A valid result may be that the proposed coverage gap is not found or that the instrument adds insufficient value.

## Public foundation: the minimal information a transformation needs

The public working paper **[The Minimal Information a Transformation Needs](https://tegrity.ai/the-minimal-information-a-transformation-needs/)** provides the field account behind the RUP hypothesis. Its starting point is simple: rationalization, automation and AI enablement, process mining, guardrails, data modernization and enterprise-architecture work all presuppose a small initiative-specific information set. At minimum, that set normally includes:

- the **boundary** of the object, process, population or portfolio slice being changed;
- an **accountable owner** able to decide, fund, accept, escalate and carry responsibility;
- an **authoritative source** or accepted canonical record from which action can proceed; and
- the **real dependencies**, costs, usage, value and risks that constrain the intervention.

The paper distinguishes information that can be retrieved from information that does not yet exist because no authorized actor has declared it. Establishing an owner, canonical source or binding boundary is therefore not only discovery: it can change responsibilities, budgets, systems and behaviour. The full precondition has three cost layers:

1. **Cost of looking** — finding existing information, sources and contradictions.
2. **Cost of deciding** — obtaining the authority and agreement needed to establish what remains undeclared.
3. **Cost of absorbing** — adapting teams, systems, budgets and operations to the consequences of those declarations.

This leads to the project's candidate predictor: initiatives facing a higher cost of clarity, greater declarative burden and more value exposed in poorly understood parts of the estate should be more likely to stall, be rescoped, under-deliver or produce a lower return. The proposition is testable; it is not presented as an established law.

## Why governance becomes the bottleneck

The working thesis **Human Intelligence Debt and the Governance Bottleneck** develops the mechanism behind that burden. It defines *architectural entropy* narrowly as having to decide about an organization's own process while basic presumed self-knowledge cannot simply be retrieved because the relevant order was never established. As systems become more entangled, decisions spanning several functions cannot be delegated cleanly and rise toward a smaller number of actors with sufficient authority. At the same time, they move away from the operational knowledge needed to make them well.

The resulting loop is causal rather than merely thematic: unresolved information and authority conditions leave people reconciling systems manually; that Human Intelligence Debt feeds shadow processes and further entanglement; entanglement centralizes more decisions; and the governance bottleneck leaves more of the debt unresolved. The thesis explicitly treats the growth of this condition as an empirical hypothesis and states observations that could falsify it.

## How the RUP project tests the idea

The OÜ JUBAP technical presentation **[The Cost of Clarity — Scientific Problem and Applied-Research Method](cost-of-clarity/cost-of-clarity-scientific-problem-and-method.pdf)** converts the field account into four non-exclusive explanations:

| Factor | Explanation | Treatment in the current study |
|---|---|---|
| **F1 — Instrument economics** | Assessment may cost more than the certainty it buys. | Measured as information-acquisition cost relative to uncertainty reduction. |
| **F2 — Incentive structure** | A useful signal may still be difficult to act on. | Recorded where observable; a full longitudinal test is deferred. |
| **F3 — Attribution convenience** | “The data was not ready” may be a generic after-the-fact explanation. | Tested against the specificity and patterning of reported gaps. |
| **F4 — Coverage** | Existing tools may answer reasonable questions while missing the load-bearing condition. | Primary explanation tested through the ex-ante/ex-post coverage gap. |

The measurable coverage construct is the set of critical items reported missing, contested, ambiguous or undeclared after an initiative, minus the items actually specified or checked before commitment. The method then moves through five stages: literature coverage mapping, elicitation of demonstrably effective practice, evidence-based audit, a sealed pre-commitment reading, and observation of the later decision and outcome.

Three hypotheses organize the work: whether a specific coverage gap exists; whether effective expert criteria can be made transferable and auditable; and whether a sealed reading adds prospective signal beyond basic initiative complexity. Reliability gates, independent evidence, simple baselines and explicit negative-result branches are intended to prevent the project from treating every result as confirmation.

## EIS and TalTech roles

**EIS / RUP** is the programme authority and assessment route. Submission for preliminary assessment is not a funding decision or technical validation.

The project design identifies two specific TalTech research groups as prospective sources of supporting R&D capacity:

- The [TalTech Information Systems Group](https://taltech.ee/en/is), for information readiness, data governance, systems architecture and large-scale information systems.
- The [TalTech Next Gen Digital State Research Group](https://taltech.ee/en/department-of-software-science/cooperation/next-gen-digital-state-research-group), for digital transformation, governance interpretation, AI architecture, requirements and implementation in public and complex institutional settings.

These are concrete intended research capabilities, not generic references to the university. Their involvement remains proposed and subject to detailed scope, staffing, quotation and agreement. Neither group is presented here as a confirmed partner, supporter, validator or contractor.

## Why it comes first

Cost of Clarity is the current applied-research priority within the [Structural Awareness Programme](https://tegrity.ai/structural-awareness-program/). It addresses the upstream problem: whether an organization has sufficient information, distinctions and authority to make a defensible commitment. If those conditions are missing or contradictory, the operating representation can be structurally incomplete from the outset. [Regime Awareness](https://tegrity.ai/series/regime-awareness-in-adaptive-systems/) then asks whether that representation remains valid as conditions change, while Minimum Sufficient Control concerns the bounded capacity needed to preserve or recover a declared objective.
